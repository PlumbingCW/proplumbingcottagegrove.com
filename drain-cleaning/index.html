When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow sink is an annoyance. A basement floor drain that gurgles when the washing machine drains is different, because that pattern points to the main line, and the next flush can push wastewater into the lowest opening in the house.

Call right away if more than one fixture is affected, if a toilet bubbles when you run a sink, or if water is rising in a tub with nothing running. Those signs mean the blockage sits past the branch lines, where a plunger cannot reach.

More than one drain is slow

Sinks and a tub that slow down together share a branch or the main line. The clog is downstream of all of them.

Gurgling and bubbling

Air pushed back through a trap means water cannot pass the blockage. A floor drain in the basement is often the first place it shows.

Water backs up into the tub

If the shower floor fills when the washer drains, the main line is restricted and the lowest fixture is taking the overflow.

Odor that comes and goes

Sewer smell near a drain can mean a dry trap, but with slow draining it usually means debris is sitting in the line.

How we clear a drain

The right tool depends on what is in the pipe. A hand auger or a small sink machine handles hair, soap scum and a kitchen blockage close to the fixture. A larger cable machine with a cutting head reaches the main line, where grease, paper and roots form the stubborn clogs.

When the cable keeps finding the same resistance, a camera shows the real cause instead of guessing. A belly in the pipe, a cracked joint or a root mass each need a different fix, and a sewer camera inspection sorts that out in one visit.

Snaking is the fit when

  • The clog is a single blockage such as hair or a paper wad
  • You want a fast, low-cost clear at one fixture
  • The line is otherwise flowing normally

Hydro jetting is the better call when

  • The same drain clogs again within months
  • Grease or scale coats the pipe wall
  • Roots have returned to a main line that was cabled before

If jetting sounds like the better answer, the hydro jetting page explains what it does and when it is safe for older cast iron.

Why drains clog in Cottage Grove homes

Cottage Grove grew fast between 1970 and 2020, from about 13,400 residents to nearly 38,800, with the biggest jumps in the 1970s and the 1990s. That means many homes carry drain lines that are now thirty to fifty years old, and mature trees planted along those same streets have had decades to find the joints.

The Mississippi River bluffs and the ravines near Ravine Regional Park also mean a lot of lots with large, thirsty trees close to the house. Root intrusion is a leading cause of main line stoppages here, and it tends to return if a line is only cabled and never cleaned back to the pipe wall.

Before we arrive

  • Stop running water in sinks, tubs and the washing machine
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into the line, because they sit in standing water and can burn skin and fixtures
  • Move rugs and stored items away from basement floor drains
  • Note which fixtures are slow so the plumber starts in the right place
  • Keep the 24/7 line handy in case water starts rising

The more you can tell us about which fixtures are affected, the faster the right tool comes off the truck.

Keeping the line clear afterward

  • Use a drain strainer in the kitchen, shower and tub
  • Wipe grease into the trash, not the sink, even with hot water
  • Flush only toilet paper and waste, not wipes sold as flushable
  • Run a hot water cycle after washing greasy dishes
  • Schedule a camera look if the same drain clogs twice in a year

If a clog keeps returning from the same fixture, the cause may be the vent, not the drain. Plumbing installation and venting work can correct a line that was never piped to drain well.

Code, permits and standards

Drain work in Minnesota sits under the Minnesota Plumbing Code. Cleaning a drain is maintenance, but cutting into a line to add a clean-out is a code item, so we follow the code's access and venting standards and tell you when a permit applies.
