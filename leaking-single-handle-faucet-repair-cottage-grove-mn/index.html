Leaking Single-Handle Faucet Repair in Cottage Grove, MN

A single-handle faucet that drips from the spout or leaks around the base wastes water and wears on your patience. The cause is almost always a worn cartridge or a set of O-rings, and both are cheap parts.

This guide shows how to find where the leak starts, what to buy, how to replace the cartridge and when a faucet is worth replacing instead of repairing.

Find where the leak is

Where it leaksLikely causeFix
Spout drips when offWorn cartridge or ceramic discReplace the cartridge
Leak at the handleWorn O-rings or sealReplace O-rings
Water at the base of the faucetLoose or worn spout O-ringsReplace the spout seals
Weak flowAerator clogged or cartridge debrisClean or replace

The location of the leak tells you which part to replace. A drip from the spout is a cartridge problem. Water that shows up at the base is usually an O-ring.

Before you start

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink, or the main water valve if they do not work
  • Plug the drain so small parts do not fall in
  • Open the faucet to release pressure
  • Lay a towel in the sink
  • Take a photo of each step so you can reassemble it

Replacing the cartridge

1

Remove the handle

Look for a small cap or set screw. Remove the cap, loosen the screw with a hex key and lift the handle off.

2

Remove the retaining part

Many faucets use a retaining nut, a clip or a cap over the cartridge. Remove it and keep it with the parts.

3

Pull out the cartridge

Grip the stem and pull straight up, wiggling if needed. Note which way it faces.

4

Install the new cartridge

Match it to the old one, lubricate the O-rings with plumber's grease and reassemble in reverse.

Cartridges are brand and model specific. Take the old one to the store or look for a number on the faucet. Buying the wrong part wastes a trip.

Ceramic disc and ball types

Some single-handle faucets use ceramic discs. These last longer, but seals around them can wear, and mineral deposits on the disc can cause leaks. Cleaning the disc with vinegar sometimes helps, and a new cartridge is the long-term fix.

Ball-type faucets have a small rotating ball with springs and seats. They wear more quickly and are repaired with a kit. If a ball faucet is old and the parts are hard to find, replacement is cheaper in the long run.

Hard water and aerators

Mineral deposits are a leading cause of faucet wear. They gum up cartridges, clog aerators and corrode seats. If your faucet loses flow gradually, unscrew the aerator at the tip of the spout and soak it in vinegar.

The extent of the problem depends on the hardness of your water supply. Test your tap or ask your water utility for its report. If the water is hard, a softener helps everything in the house last longer, as discussed in water softener service.

Repair or replace

Repair

  • The faucet is in good shape
  • Parts are available
  • The finish is fine
  • The cost is small

Replace

  • Corrosion or cracks on the body
  • Parts are discontinued
  • The faucet is decades old
  • You want a different style

Kitchen faucets with a pull-down spray

Kitchen faucets add a hose, a diverter and sometimes a weighted hose guide. A leak under the sink along the hose usually means a loose connection at the quick-connect fitting, which can be reseated. A leak at the spout when the spray is on may mean the diverter is failing.

Pull-down faucets sit on a deck with mounting nuts underneath. If the faucet wobbles, tighten the mounting nuts from below. A loose faucet puts stress on the supply lines and can open small leaks.

Reassembly tips

  • Lay parts out in order on a towel as you remove them
  • Lightly grease O-rings with plumber's grease before reinstalling
  • Install the cartridge facing the same direction as the old one
  • Hand-tighten the retaining nut, then snug it a quarter turn
  • Turn the water back on slowly and check for drips

Open the faucet and let it run for a minute to flush debris from the new cartridge. If the leak continues, the seat or the faucet body may be worn, and replacement may make more sense.
