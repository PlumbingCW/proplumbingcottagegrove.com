What makes Old Cottage Grove different

The city's own historic preservation chapter describes the old village area and the effort to protect its character. Several local sites are recognized there, including the John P. Furber House at 7310 Lamar Avenue South, built in 1871 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1982, and the John Watson House on 70th Street South, added to the city register in 1984.

For a plumber, history has one practical meaning. Houses in a long-settled area have usually been remodeled more than once, so the pipes behind one wall can be from a different decade than the pipes behind the next. Age tells us where to look, but it does not tell us what is installed. We find that out by looking, and we do not guess from the year a home was built.

Plumbing problems we are called for here

Slow or repeated drain backups

A line that backs up more than once points to something in the pipe, not just a clog at the fixture. Drain cleaning clears it, and a camera look through sewer inspection shows the cause.

Water heater trouble

A tank that leaks, rumbles or runs out of hot water quickly needs a look. See water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Rising bills or damp spots

Water use that climbs with no change in habits can mean a hidden leak. Leak detection finds it without opening more wall than needed.

Old fixtures and valves

A shutoff that will not turn or a faucet that drips for good is often a worn part. Faucet repair and toilet repair handle the common ones.

Working in an older or remodeled house

When we open up a fixture in an older house, we expect surprises and plan around them. We check the shutoff first, protect finished floors and walls and tell you what we find before we change anything.

If your home is on a historic register, the city is the right place to ask whether any planned work needs review. Most plumbing repairs happen inside the walls, but a question to the city costs nothing and avoids delay.

Before the plumber arrives
