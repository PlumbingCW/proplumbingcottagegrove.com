A railroad village inside the city

Local histories describe Langdon as a station on the railroad, with a depot built in 1869. The community was platted in 1871 by J. T. Dodge and later had a creamery, built in 1875. Today it is a part of Cottage Grove, not a separate town.

The practical point is that we do not assume how an address is connected. Water and waste connections are worth confirming house by house.

Ask first: city utilities or private systems?

Before a plumber can plan a repair, it helps to know how your home is connected. Some homes use city water and sewer. Others may use a private well or a septic system, and we do not assume which applies to your address.

The City of Cottage Grove can tell you what serves your lot. When you call, mention a well, a septic tank or a lift station if you have one, so the plumber arrives with the right tools.

Common calls from Langdon addresses

Drain backups

A slow drain or a sewer smell in the basement calls for drain cleaning. If it comes back, a camera look through sewer inspection shows what is in the line.

Basement water

Water on a basement floor can come from a failed pump. See sump pump service and basement waterproofing.

Water heater problems

Cold showers or a wet floor near the tank need attention. See water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Hard water

If you see scale on fixtures, ask about water softener service. We test or ask about your water before suggesting equipment.
