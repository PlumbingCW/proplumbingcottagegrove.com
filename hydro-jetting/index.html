What hydro jetting is, in plain terms

Hydro jetting pushes high-pressure water through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets backward and forward. The water scours the inside wall of the pipe and carries debris toward the sewer instead of poking a hole through it.

A cable only cuts a path. Jetting cleans the whole circumference of the pipe, which is why it is the standard fix for grease, scale and fine root growth that cabling leaves behind.

Sewer backup emergency: when a cable is not enough

Same clog, same drain

A drain that backs up every few months has a coating problem, not a one-time blockage.

Main line slows after rain

Roots that enter at joints swell with moisture, so flow gets worse after wet weather.

Kitchen line that slows in winter

Grease hardens as the pipe cools, which is why cold months bring restaurant and kitchen backups.

Cable keeps hitting resistance

If the cable stops at the same spot each time, look at the pipe with a camera before cutting further.

When a backup is an emergency

Sewage coming up through a floor drain or tub is a health issue as well as damage. Stop using water, keep children and pets away from the area and call the 24/7 line.

We can dispatch for the same day when available, confirm the plan and the price before work starts, and start with a camera if the line has a history of trouble.

Where jetting is not the right tool

Jetting is not for every pipe. A cracked or collapsed clay line, a badly bellied section or a separated joint can leave debris behind or push water into the soil. In those cases a camera first prevents damage, and the answer may be a sewer line repair instead.

Very fragile cast iron, common in older homes, calls for lower pressure or a different method. If we think jetting is a bad idea for your pipe, we will say so rather than sell it.

Jetting and the Cottage Grove sewer service

In most Cottage Grove neighborhoods the house sewer runs from the foundation to a city main in the street. The city owns the main, and you generally own the lateral from your house to it, which is why a backup on your side of the connection is your repair.

If you are unsure where your responsibility ends, ask City of Cottage Grove Public Works before spending money. A line with roots from older boulevard trees is the usual case where jetting pays off, followed by a camera check to see whether the joint needs repair.

What to do before jetting day

  • Clear the area around the clean-out or the basement floor drain
  • Stop laundry and dishwasher use on the morning of the visit
  • Tell the plumber about any past repairs, liners or camera reports
  • Plan on water being off to the affected fixtures for a short time
  • Ask what the camera shows once the line is clear

Preventing a repeat

After a jetting, the line is as clean as it will get. Keeping it that way means controlling what goes in. Grease belongs in a can, wipes belong in the trash and a strainer belongs in every kitchen and shower drain.

If roots were the cause, plan a follow-up camera check on a schedule, so you catch regrowth before the next backup. That is cheaper than waiting for a weekend emergency.

Code, permits and standards

Jetting is a maintenance method, not a code-defined repair, so the standards that matter are the pipe manufacturer's guidelines for pressure and the condition shown on camera. If jetting reveals a defect that needs repair, that repair falls under the Minnesota Plumbing Code.
