Overflowing toilet emergency: stop it first

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water entering the bowl
  2. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise
  3. Do not flush again
  4. Use towels to protect the floor and keep people away from sewage
  5. Call the 24/7 line if the toilet will not drain or the shutoff will not close

If more than one drain is affected, the problem is probably the main line and not the toilet. See sewer line cleaning for what that involves.

When the base is the problem

A toilet that leaks at the floor is sitting on a failed seal or a damaged flange. The flange is the ring that connects the toilet to the drain and anchors it to the floor. Over time it can crack, corrode or loosen, and water seeps into the subfloor.

A soft floor around the toilet means water has been getting in for a while. Pulling the toilet shows the flange, the floor and the wax ring, and we fix what we find. A new wax ring alone will not hold if the flange is broken.

Repair

  • Flapper, fill valve or handle replaced
  • Wax ring and bolts reset
  • Often done in one visit

Replace

  • Cracked bowl or tank
  • Repeated clogs from an old design
  • Water use or comfort upgrade

Toilet installation done right

What a proper installation includes

  • New wax ring or an approved seal
  • Closet bolts set and tightened evenly
  • A flange that sits level at or just above the finished floor
  • A new supply line and shutoff valve
  • A test flush and a check for leaks
  • Caulk or a gap at the base only if code and the manufacturer allow

We remove and haul away the old toilet when we replace it.

Toilets in Cottage Grove homes

Homes from the 1970s through the 1990s often have toilets that use more water than a modern fixture. Replacing one can cut water use and make flushing stronger. Check the gallons per flush rating, and ask about models sized to your rough-in.

In basement bathrooms, a toilet may sit on an ejector system. If a basement toilet gurgles or will not flush, the problem may be the ejector pit, not the fixture. We check before replacing anything.

Habits that keep toilets working

  • Flush only toilet paper and waste
  • Use a plunger designed for toilets, not a sink plunger
  • Replace the flapper every few years
  • Check the shutoff valve by turning it gently once a year
  • Do not use in-tank bleach tablets, which wear rubber parts

Several toilets are installed during bathroom remodels. If you are adding a bathroom, plumbing installation covers the rough-in and venting.

Code, permits and standards

A repair inside the tank is routine, while resetting or replacing a toilet is plumbing work under the Minnesota Plumbing Code. Toilet flow rates are set by federal and state standards, so check the gallons per flush rating before you buy a new fixture.
