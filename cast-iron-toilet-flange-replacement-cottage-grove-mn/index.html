Replace a Cast Iron Toilet Flange: Cottage Grove, MN Guide

The toilet flange is the ring that connects your toilet to the drain and anchors it to the floor. When it cracks, rusts or breaks, the toilet rocks, leaks at the base or smells, and the floor underneath can rot.

This guide explains how to tell if the flange is the problem, what options exist for a cast iron drain and why some jobs are better left to a plumber.

Signs the flange has failed

Toilet rocks

The bolts are loose or the flange ears are broken.

Water at the base

The wax ring cannot seal against a damaged or low flange.

Sewer smell

A bad seal lets gas escape.

Soft or stained floor

Long-term leaks have rotted the subfloor.

If you see any of these, shut off the water at the valve behind the toilet and plan to pull the toilet. Do not keep using a toilet that rocks, since the seal will fail.

Types of flanges

TypeWhere you see itNotes
Cast iron flangeOlder homes with cast iron drainsPart of the pipe or attached with lead and oakum
PVC or ABS flangeNewer homesGlued to plastic pipe
Brass or lead flangeOlder plumbingCan corrode or crack
Repair ringRetrofitBolted over a damaged flange

A cast iron flange is a hub on the end of the pipe or a separate piece caulked into the hub. Replacing it can mean cutting the pipe, which is hard work in a tight space.

Your options for a damaged flange

Repair ring

  • Fits over a damaged flange
  • Bolts to the subfloor
  • Keeps the pipe in place
  • Good when the flange is intact but the ears are broken

Full replacement

  • Cuts and replaces the flange or pipe
  • Needed when the flange is badly corroded or detached
  • May involve a repair coupling
  • Best left to a plumber with cast iron experience

Cast iron work is harder than plastic. Cutting it needs a snap cutter or a saw, and old lead and oakum joints can contain lead. If your flange is attached with lead caulk, treat it carefully and ask a plumber.

How the work goes

1

Pull the toilet

Shut off the water, drain the tank and bowl, disconnect the supply line and lift the toilet off the bolts.

2

Inspect

Check the flange for cracks, rust and height, and check the subfloor for soft spots.

3

Repair or replace

Install a repair ring or replace the flange, and fix any rotted subfloor first.

4

Reset the toilet

Install a new wax ring and bolts, set the toilet and tighten evenly.

Flange height matters

The flange should sit at or slightly above the finished floor. A flange that is too low, often after new tile or flooring is added, leaves a gap that the wax ring cannot fill. The fix is a spacer or an extender, or a raised flange.

Check the height after any flooring change. It is a common cause of a toilet that leaks after a remodel.

When to call a plumber

If the floor is soft, the pipe is loose or the flange is lead and oakum, a plumber should handle it. A bad repair can leak into the subfloor for months before anyone notices.

If you see sewage or the toilet will not drain, treat it as urgent and call the 24/7 line.

Tools and materials

  • A replacement flange or repair ring sized for your pipe
  • Stainless steel screws and closet bolts
  • A new wax ring or a wax-free seal
  • Pry bar, putty knife and a hacksaw or reciprocating saw for subfloor repair
  • Rags, gloves and a respirator for old debris
  • A level to check the height and the toilet seat

Common mistakes

  • Stacking wax rings to fill a gap, which fails over time
  • Using screws that are too short to hold the ring in solid subfloor
  • Forcing a flange into a pipe that is not round
  • Overtightening bolts and cracking the base of the toilet
  • Skipping the subfloor repair when the wood is soft

The floor underneath is the foundation of the repair. A flange screwed into rotten plywood will come loose again, so cut out and replace damaged subfloor before you set anything on top of it.

Cottage Grove note: older homes and inspections

Homes with cast iron drains are typically older ones. Work on those drains falls under the Minnesota Plumbing Code, and city inspectors handle inspections where the city is the administrative authority. Follow the flange manufacturer's guidelines for bolt spacing and seal type.
