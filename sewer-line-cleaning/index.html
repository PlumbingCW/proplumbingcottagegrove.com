Main line clogs: the signs to take seriously

Basement floor drain overflows

The floor drain sits at the lowest point of the system, so it shows a main line clog first.

Toilet gurgles when a sink drains

Air displaced by water that cannot flow past the clog finds the nearest trap.

Tub fills when the washer runs

Laundry water pushes into the lowest tub or shower when the main line is blocked.

Every drain is slow

If sinks, showers and toilets all slow together, the problem is past the branches.

The difference between a main line clog and a single drain problem is how many fixtures are affected. Sewer line cleaning is the work done at the main, usually through a clean-out outside the house or at the basement stack.

How the line gets cleaned

1

Find the clean-out

Most homes have a capped clean-out near the foundation or in the basement. If there is none, a toilet may need to be pulled.

2

Cable the line

A powered cable with a cutting head works through grease, paper and root growth until water drains freely.

3

Camera the result

A short camera run shows whether the pipe is sound or the clog was a symptom of damage.

4

Report and plan

You get a plain account of what was found and whether a follow-up cleaning or repair makes sense.

Roots, grease and wipes: the usual culprits

Cottage Grove has plenty of mature trees along its older streets, and roots find sewer joints on their own. A root mass in a clay or older iron line can hold back paper and lead to a full stoppage. Cutting the roots clears the line, but roots grow back, so a camera check months later is cheaper than another emergency.

Wipes are the second big cause. Products sold as flushable often do not break down, and they catch on roots and joints. The third is grease, which cools in the line and builds a coating that cables skip over. That is where hydro jetting gets better results.

Cable cleaning

  • Fast and cost-effective for a fresh stoppage
  • Good at cutting roots in the pipe
  • Leaves residue on the pipe wall

Hydro jetting

  • Washes the pipe wall clean
  • Removes grease and scale
  • Needs a pipe that is sound enough for pressure

Why a backup returns

If a line was cleaned and backs up again in a few months, the problem is almost always in the pipe, not the cleaning. A belly holds water and paper, a joint lets in roots and a collapsed section stops everything.

That is where a camera earns its price. It turns a repeating nuisance into a specific, fixable defect, and it is the first step before any sewer line repair discussion.

Preparing for the visit

  • Locate the clean-out or tell us where the basement floor drain is
  • Stop using water until the line is cleared
  • Move anything stored near the clean-out
  • Keep pets and children away from the work area
  • Tell us if the line was cleaned before and when

If you have a camera report from a prior visit, share it. It saves time and avoids repeating work.

Who owns what in Cottage Grove

The sewer main in the street is the city's. The pipe from the house to the main is usually the homeowner's. A blockage on your side is yours to clear, while a problem in the street main is a call to City of Cottage Grove Public Works.

If neighbors are also having trouble, the main may be the issue. Calling public works first is free and can save you a cleaning visit.

Code, permits and standards

Cleaning a line is routine, but any new clean-out or pipe change that follows is governed by the Minnesota Plumbing Code. We note what the camera shows so you have a record if a repair or a permit follows.
