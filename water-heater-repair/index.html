Water heater emergency: no hot water or a leaking tank

  1. Locate the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the heater and turn it clockwise to stop the flow
  2. For an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For gas, turn the control to pilot or off
  3. Never touch the unit if water is around electrical outlets
  4. Move stored items away from the tank and put a bucket under any drip
  5. Call the 24/7 line so a plumber can see whether it is a repair or a replacement

Most tank leaks that come from the tank itself are not repairable. A drip from a fitting, a valve or a pipe connection often is, so a quick look is worth the visit.

Repair or replace: the honest test

Repair makes sense when

  • The tank is not leaking from its shell
  • The heater is under about ten years old
  • A single part such as an element, thermostat or igniter has failed
  • The repair costs much less than a new unit

Replace makes sense when

  • Water is escaping from the tank body
  • The heater is past its expected service life
  • Repeated repairs add up
  • Rust or sediment shows the tank is failing inside

If the verdict is replace, the next step is a water heater replacement, and we explain tank size and efficiency before anything is ordered. You should not be pressured into a new unit when a part will do.

Why water heaters fail faster in the east metro

Where water carries dissolved minerals, they settle as sediment in the bottom of a tank, where they insulate the water from the burner or element. The heater works harder, noisier and eventually fails sooner. Ask City of Cottage Grove Public Works for the local water report or test your tap, since hardness varies by area and well field.

Winter makes a failing heater obvious quickly. Incoming water is cold, so a weak element or a dirty burner cannot keep up, and the first sign is often a shower that never gets warm. Drain and flush maintenance every year or two slows that down, and an inspection of the anode rod tells you how much life is left.

Safety issues that cannot wait

Gas smell or carbon monoxide alarm

If you smell gas near a gas heater, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber. Do not use switches or phones inside.

A carbon monoxide alarm that sounds near a gas heater means leave the home and call 911. A plumber checks venting afterward, once it is safe.

Before the plumber arrives

  • Find the model and age sticker on the tank
  • Shut off the cold water supply if there is a leak
  • Clear a path to the heater
  • Write down what you noticed, such as noises or temperature changes
  • Keep pets away from the area

The sticker on the tank gives age and capacity, which helps us bring the right part.

Preventing the next failure

  • Flush the tank every year or two to remove sediment
  • Check the temperature and pressure relief valve for drips
  • Inspect the anode rod on older tanks
  • Set the thermostat at a moderate temperature that works for your household
  • Ask about an expansion tank if your system has a closed supply

A dripping relief valve deserves attention. If it only drips near the top of its range, the cause may be pressure, which is something a plumber measures before deciding whether to replace the valve or add an expansion tank.

Code, permits and standards

Repairs follow the manufacturer's service guidelines, and a temperature and pressure relief valve must stay in place, correctly rated and piped to a safe discharge. Water heater work falls under the Minnesota Plumbing Code where a part is replaced.
