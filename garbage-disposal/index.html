Disposal emergency: a leak or a jam, what to do first

  1. Switch the disposal off, and unplug it or flip its breaker before reaching in
  2. Never put your hand into the chamber
  3. Use a long tool or tongs to remove visible objects such as a utensil or bone
  4. Try the reset button on the bottom of the unit
  5. Call if it hums but will not spin, trips the breaker or leaks

Most disposals have a hex socket in the bottom that lets you turn the plate by hand with a wrench, which frees a simple jam. If that does not work, the motor or a seal may have failed.

Repair or replace

Repair is sensible when

  • The unit is only jammed
  • A reset or a wire is the problem
  • A hose or flange seal is leaking
  • The disposal is fairly new

Replace is best when

  • The housing is cracked
  • The motor has burned out
  • It has leaked repeatedly
  • The unit is more than about a decade old

Installing a new disposal

A new disposal mounts under the sink flange, connects to the drain trap and plugs into an outlet or is hardwired. It also needs a connection to the dishwasher drain if you have one. A knockout plug inside the disposal must be removed when the dishwasher connects, which is a step that is often missed in DIY installs and causes the dishwasher to back up.

Horsepower matters less than people expect. A mid-range unit suits most households, and a larger one handles heavy use better. The sink has to be able to take a single or double bowl, and cabinet space under the sink decides what fits.

What a proper installation includes

  • New mounting assembly and sink flange seal
  • Correct drain trap alignment
  • Knockout removed and dishwasher hose connected when applicable
  • A power connection that meets code, with a switch within reach
  • A test run and leak check

If your unit is hardwired, the electrical work should meet local code.

Kitchen drains in older Cottage Grove homes

Kitchen drains from the 1970s and 1980s may be steel or older plastic with scale on the inside. Grease and starchy foods can narrow them, and a disposal pushes more material into a pipe that may already be tight. If the sink drains slowly even with the disposal running, the line beyond it may be the real problem. See drain cleaning for what that involves.

Using a disposal well

  • Run cold water before, during and after use
  • Avoid grease, coffee grounds, bones, stringy vegetables and pasta or rice
  • Never put utensils in the unit
  • Clean with ice and citrus peels or a manufacturer-approved cleaner
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners with a disposal

Code, permits and standards

A disposal connects to power and to the drain, so both the Minnesota Plumbing Code and electrical rules matter. The manufacturer's guidelines decide the wiring method, and we follow them and the local rules for hardwired units.
