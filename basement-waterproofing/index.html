The first question is whether the water is coming from outside, from a pipe or from humid air. Treating the wrong source wastes money, so a plumber looks at all three.

Interior drainage, explained

An interior drain system sits along the inside edge of the foundation, either in a channel cut into the floor or beneath it. Water that reaches the footing flows into the channel and runs to a sump pit, where a pump sends it outside.

It manages water pressure from below rather than trying to hold it back. For a home with a history of seepage at the floor-wall joint, it is a common plumbing-side fix, and it works with the sump pump you already have or a new one.

The Cottage Grove ground conditions

The city sits above the Mississippi River in a part of the metro where bluffs and ravines meet the river valley. Ravine Regional Park, with its 515 acres, is one reminder that the land here is cut by drainage paths. Water follows the same paths under and around houses.

Homes near the river and lower areas may sit closer to the water table, and spring melt can raise it. Soils vary from sandy to clayey, so what drains well on one lot may not drain on the next. Ask a plumber to look at the actual pit and the actual basement, not a generic plan.

What to note before we arrive

  • Where water appears and how long it takes after rain
  • Whether the sump has been running more than usual
  • Any past repairs, membranes or patches
  • The age of the home and the sump pump
  • Gutters, downspouts and grading around the foundation

A photo of each wet area helps a lot.

What plumbers do and do not do

Plumbing side

Other trades

  • Foundation crack injection and structural repair
  • Exterior excavation and membranes
  • Grading and landscape drainage
  • Mold remediation

Foundation cracks and exterior waterproofing are other trades. We tell you if the cause of your water is outside plumbing, and we do not sell work that will not fix the problem. If water is entering because a pump has failed, sump pump repair is the first call.

What to expect after the fix

Interior drainage and a good sump can keep a basement dry in most conditions. They do not guarantee a perfectly dry space, particularly during extreme events. Keep gutters clean, downspouts extended and the sump tested.

A dehumidifier helps with dampness that does not come from seepage. Finish only what you are prepared to protect, and avoid storing valuables on the floor in a basement that has had water before.

Signs the problem is plumbing and not seepage

  • The water appears with no rain or snowmelt
  • The meter moves when every fixture is off
  • Moisture is concentrated near a pipe, water heater or washer
  • The sump pump runs on a dry day
  • A floor drain bubbles when upstairs water drains

In each case the answer is different from interior drainage. A supply leak is a repair. A drain backup is a cleaning or a repair. We sort out which one you have before suggesting anything larger.

Code, permits and standards

The plumbing side of waterproofing, such as a sump pit and discharge, follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code. Structural repair and exterior waterproofing follow different rules and different trades, so we tell you plainly when a job is not ours.
