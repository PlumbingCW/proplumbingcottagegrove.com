What to do in a plumbing emergency

An emergency plumber cannot always be there in the first minute, so the first minutes belong to you. These four steps limit damage while you wait for a licensed plumber in Cottage Grove.

Shut off the water

Turn the main valve clockwise. It is usually in the basement near the meter or where the line enters the house.

Stop using drains

If a drain is backing up, stop running water and keep chemical openers out of the line.

Protect the electrical

If water is near outlets or the panel, stay clear and use the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher what is happening and what you have shut off, so the right plumber and tools come.

Plumbing in Cottage Grove: what the housing tells us

Cottage Grove sits on the north bank of the Mississippi River in Washington County, about ten miles from downtown Saint Paul. The city grew from roughly 13,400 residents in 1970 to nearly 38,800 in 2020, with the fastest growth in the 1970s and 1990s, so most homes here are thirty to fifty years old.

That age matters. Drain lines from the 1970s and 1980s meet root growth from mature trees. Water heaters installed in the 1990s are reaching the end of their lives. Some homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may contain polybutylene supply pipe, which is known to fail at fittings.

The land matters too. Ravine Regional Park, with its 515 acres off Keats Avenue South, is one example of how bluffs and ravines cut through the city. Basements on lower lots and near the river can see groundwater rise during spring melt, which is why sump pump condition is one of the first things we check.

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, and according to the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, required inspections must be performed. We follow the manufacturer's installation standards and the permit steps your city requires.
