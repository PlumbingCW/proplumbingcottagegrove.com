Hastings housing by era: what is behind the walls

Hastings was founded in 1853 and incorporated in 1857. The East Second Street Commercial Historic District and the West Second Street Residential Historic District still anchor downtown, and the old courthouse finished in 1871 now serves as City Hall. Buildings and homes of that age commonly carry plumbing that has been patched and updated over decades.

If you live in an older house near downtown, expect a mix of materials. Original cast iron or clay drains, galvanized supply lines and copper repairs can all sit in one house. Our job is to find which failed, then fix it without disturbing more than necessary.

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak: before we arrive

Shut off the main valve

Find the main shutoff, usually in the basement near the meter, and turn it clockwise. In older Hastings homes it may be a gate valve that is stiff, so turn it slowly and do not force it.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, the panel or appliances, switch off the breaker only when you can reach it dry. Otherwise stay clear and call.

Clear the area

Lift belongings off the floor, move rugs and keep children and pets away. Take photos for insurance before cleanup.

Water, wells and what to ask the city

Hastings supplies water from groundwater wells. The city's 2025 drinking water report describes six wells drawing from the Jordan aquifer, and it notes that treatment to remove PFAS is being built. It does not give a hardness number, so we do not either.

The practical point for your plumbing is simple. Mineral content affects water heaters and fixtures, and PFAS is a drinking-water question for the city and the Minnesota Department of Health. Hastings Public Works can answer both, and a plumber can then match any filter or softener to what the report says. See water softener and filtration service for how that works.

Near the rivers: why Hastings basements get wet

Hastings sits where the Vermillion River falls toward the Mississippi, with the Lock and Dam No. 2 on the river just northwest of downtown. Low ground near water means a higher water table, and a sump pump in that kind of basement works hard in spring.

A battery backup is worth considering if your pit has ever filled during a storm. Power outages often arrive with the heaviest rain, which is when a pump matters most.

Code, permits and standards

According to the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, plumbing installations must meet the Minnesota Plumbing Code and required inspections must be performed. Where the City of Hastings is the administrative authority, its inspectors handle plumbing inspections, so ask Hastings city staff which permits apply to your address. We follow the manufacturer's installation standards and the permit steps your city requires.
