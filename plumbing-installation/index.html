Basement bathrooms: a common east metro project

A lot of Cottage Grove homes were built with unfinished basements, and adding a bathroom is a popular upgrade. The challenge is that the drain may sit above the sewer line, so waste cannot flow by gravity. A sewage ejector pump solves it by lifting waste to the main drain.

If the existing slab has to be cut to run a drain, the plumber coordinates the work and marks the layout first. Ask whether your basement has a floor drain at a usable depth, and whether the sewer line is accessible. A camera check before you plan helps avoid surprises.

Venting, in plain language

Every fixture drain needs air behind it, or water leaves with a gurgle and traps can be siphoned empty. A vent stack through the roof gives the system air, and branch vents connect individual fixtures to it. A fixture placed too far from a vent, or with an undersized vent, will drain slowly and smell.

Venting is the usual reason a DIY or a poorly planned remodel fails inspection. We plan the vent at the start, so the walls do not have to be reopened later.

Permits and the Minnesota Plumbing Code

Plumbing work in Minnesota follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, and licensed plumbers are credentialed through the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry. Many projects need a permit and an inspection from your city. We tell you what applies to your job and handle the permit steps.

Cities set their own permit procedures, so ask the Cottage Grove building department about your property if you are unsure. Keeping the permit record helps at resale, when buyers and inspectors may ask for it.
