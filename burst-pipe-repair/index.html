When a burst pipe is an emergency

A burst pipe is always an emergency if water is flowing or spreading. Every minute raises the cost of repair, since water reaches insulation, drywall and electrical systems. Frozen pipes that have not yet burst are also urgent, because a thaw can release the water suddenly.

The first thing to do is stop the water. Find the main shutoff, usually in the basement near the water meter or where the pipe enters the house, and turn it fully clockwise.

Why pipes burst in a Minnesota winter

Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure builds between the ice and a closed valve or faucet. The split often forms between the ice blockage and a closed faucet, so it can be some distance from where the freeze began.

Minnesota winters bring prolonged subzero stretches. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawlspaces and attics are most at risk, and so are lines near a drafty rim joist or a window well. After a long cold spell, a pipe can split quietly and show up as a stain days later.

Frozen but not burst: what to do

If a pipe is frozen

  • Keep the faucet open so water can flow as the ice melts
  • Warm the pipe gradually with a hair dryer or heating pad, never an open flame
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls
  • Check other fixtures for no flow, since the freeze may extend
  • Call for help if you cannot find the frozen spot

If you see a bulge, a crack or water dripping, shut off the main and call right away.

After the water stops

Water damage grows with time, so drying matters. Remove wet carpet and pad, open walls if water is trapped and run fans or dehumidifiers. We repair the pipe, and a restoration company handles the drying and mold prevention. Take photos before cleanup for your insurance claim.

Check your homeowners policy. Many cover sudden bursts but not slow leaks, and coverage can depend on whether the home was heated. Your insurer decides, so call them early.

Preventing the next freeze

  • Insulate pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawlspaces
  • Seal drafts around rim joists and where pipes enter the house
  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor spigots before winter
  • Keep the heat on if you leave town, set no lower than a safe temperature
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test it each year

Code, permits and standards

A burst pipe repair must meet the Minnesota Plumbing Code like any other pipe work, and required inspections still apply. Emergency repairs get documented, and we give you the details for your insurer. Ask your city whether a permit follows an emergency repair.
