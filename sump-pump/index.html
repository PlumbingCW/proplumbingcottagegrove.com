When a failed sump pump is an emergency

A sump pit collects groundwater from the drain tile around your foundation and the pump sends it outside. If the pump fails, the pit overflows and the water goes to the lowest part of the basement. In a storm or a snowmelt week, that can happen fast.

If water is rising and the pump is not running, check that it is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped. If it still will not run, call. If water is near outlets, stay away from the area and use the breaker panel only if you can reach it dry.

Pump runs constantly

A pump that never shuts off may be undersized, stuck or facing heavy groundwater.

Pump does not start

A failed switch, a seized motor or a dead power supply.

Strange noises

Grinding or rattling often means a worn impeller or debris.

Water in the pit never drops

The check valve may have failed, sending water back into the pit.

Backup systems: the part many homes skip

Storms often take out power at the same time the sump pump is needed. A battery backup pump or a water-powered unit keeps the pit from overflowing during an outage. A high-water alarm adds a warning when the water is rising and you might not hear the pump.

Backup systems need testing. A battery that was installed years ago may not hold a charge, and a plumber can check it during a pump service call.

Sump pump maintenance

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump start
  • Clean debris from the pit and the intake screen
  • Check that the discharge line runs well away from the foundation
  • Test the battery backup and alarm
  • Make sure the check valve is installed and working

Test before the spring thaw and before heavy storm season.

Discharge lines and local rules

Sump discharge should flow away from the house and should not go into the sanitary sewer. Many cities prohibit connecting a sump to the sewer because it can overload the system. Ask the City of Cottage Grove what applies to your property before changing a discharge path.

In winter, a discharge line can freeze at the outlet. A freeze-resistant discharge fitting or a longer run to a point where water can drain keeps the line open. See basement waterproofing for drain tile and interior drainage work.

Code, permits and standards

Sump pump installation touches the Minnesota Plumbing Code, electrical rules and local drainage rules. Many cities also set standards for where sump water may discharge, so ask the City of Cottage Grove before you change the discharge path.
